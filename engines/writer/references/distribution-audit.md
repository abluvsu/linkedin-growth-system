# Distribution Audit Framework

> **Pipeline Position:** Second-to-last stage — runs after the post is drafted, before final output.
> **Purpose:** Score the post's viral mechanics across 5 dimensions. A high-quality post can still die on distribution if it doesn't trigger the right sharing behaviors.

---

## How to Use This Audit

After drafting a post (hook, body, CTA finalized), score it 1–5 on each of the 5 dimensions below. Use the guidance under each dimension to diagnose weak spots and improve them before publishing.

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 1 | No signal — this dimension is completely absent |
| 2 | Weak — present but unlikely to trigger action |
| 3 | Moderate — some people will act, but it's not optimized |
| 4 | Strong — clear trigger for this behavior in most readers |
| 5 | Exceptional — this dimension alone could carry the post |

**Minimum threshold:** Total score ≥ 16/25 to publish. If below 16, revise the weakest dimension before posting. No single dimension should score below 2.

---

## Dimension 1: Comment Potential

**Score range:** 1–5

### What Drives It

Comments come from disagreement, personal resonance, or the desire to be seen. Posts that invite comments share one or more of these traits:

- **Polarizing takes** — "Cold outreach is dead" forces people to defend their position
- **Experience prompts** — "What's the worst advice you received early in your career?" pulls stories out of readers
- **Specificity that triggers "well, actually..."** — Concrete claims ("I grew from 0 to 10K followers in 90 days doing X") invite people to challenge, question, or add nuance
- **Ranked lists** — "The 3 most overrated tools in marketing" makes people argue about what's missing
- **Vulnerable admissions** — "I got fired and it was the best thing that happened to me" creates psychological safety for others to share their own stories
- **Unfinished thoughts** — Ending with a genuine question ("I'm not sure if this is right — what's your experience?") lowers the barrier to respond

### What Kills It

- **Unassailable truisms** — "Hard work pays off" leaves nothing to add
- **Exhaustive posts** — If you've covered every angle, there's nothing left for the audience to contribute
- **Closed-ended CTAs** — "Agree?" gets likes, not comments. "What would you add?" gets comments
- **Academic tone** — Formal, detached writing discourages personal responses
- **No clear gap** — If the post feels complete, readers scroll past

### How to Improve a Low Score

1. Remove one point from your argument and let the audience fill it in
2. Add a specific claim that reasonable people could disagree with
3. End with an open question that requires a personal answer, not a yes/no
4. Insert a line like "I might be wrong about this, but..." to lower the reply barrier
5. Reference a common experience and ask "Did this happen to you too?"

## Dimension 2: Save Potential

**Score range:** 1–5

### What Drives It

Saves come from utility. People save content they plan to revisit — during a project, a job search, a hard conversation. High-save posts share these traits:

- **Step-by-step processes** — "How I audit a landing page in 15 minutes (7 steps)" is inherently saveable
- **Checklists** — "Before you hit publish on your next post, check these 9 things"
- **Frameworks with names** — A titled framework ("The 3C Positioning Test") feels like intellectual property worth keeping
- **Templates and fill-in-the-blank structures** — "Use this email template when following up after a warm intro"
- **Reference lists** — "12 tools I use to run a one-person agency" becomes a bookmark
- **Swipe files** — Collections of hooks, CTAs, subject lines, or scripts

### What Kills It

- **Pure opinion** — "I think AI will change everything" has no revisit value
- **Vague advice** — "Be more strategic" can't be applied later
- **Time-sensitive content** — "LinkedIn just changed the algorithm" becomes stale
- **No structure** — Wall-of-text posts with no numbered steps, bullets, or clear sections don't feel saveable
- **Content that requires the author** — If the insight only makes sense in context, it can't be reused

### How to Improve a Low Score

1. Convert your insight into a numbered process (even 3 steps works)
2. Add a named framework — give your mental model a title
3. Include at least one template, script, or fill-in-the-blank element
4. Structure with clear headers or numbered lists so it scans as a reference
5. Ask: "Would I bookmark this if someone else wrote it?" If no, add utility

## Dimension 3: Share Potential

**Score range:** 1–5

### What Drives It

Shares (reposts and DM forwards) come from two motives: practical utility ("my coworker needs to see this") and identity signaling ("I want people to know I think this way"). High-share posts have:

- **Practical tools others need** — "Here's the exact spreadsheet I use to track client projects" gets forwarded to teams
- **Industry truth-telling** — "Nobody in SaaS wants to admit this, but..." gets shared as "finally someone said it"
- **Identity-signaling statements** — Posts that let the sharer say "this is what I believe" by proxy
- **Impressive data or results** — "We cut churn by 40% with one change" gets shared as proof of a strategy
- **Content that makes the sharer look smart** — If forwarding your post makes someone look informed, they'll share it
- **Tribal rallying cries** — Posts that unite a group ("Freelancers are the most underrated operators in tech") get reshared within that tribe

### What Kills It

- **Too personal** — "My journey from burnout to balance" is interesting but hard to share without it feeling weird
- **Too niche** — If only 50 people understand it, the share pool is tiny
- **No clear takeaway** — If the sharer can't explain why they're sharing it, they won't
- **Aggressive self-promotion** — "I'm launching my course" never gets shared
- **Content that requires full reading** — If the value isn't clear from the hook and first 2 lines, people won't risk sharing something their audience might not finish

### How to Improve a Low Score

1. Add a clear, practical takeaway that someone could forward with "you need to see this"
2. Include a tribal identity signal — name the group this post serves
3. Make the hook strong enough that the sharer feels confident the recipient will read it
4. Add a data point or result that makes the content feel evidence-based
5. Ask: "Would I send this to a specific person I know?" If you can name someone, the share potential is high

## Dimension 4: Profile Click Potential

**Score range:** 1–5

### What Drives It

Profile clicks are the most valuable engagement for business outcomes — they convert attention into leads, followers, and clients. Posts that drive profile visits share these traits:

- **Demonstrated expertise** — Not "I know about sales," but showing a specific, advanced technique that makes readers think "who IS this person?"
- **Insider knowledge** — "After running 200 client onboardings, here's what I noticed..." signals depth that can't be faked
- **Unexpected authority** — A post about AI from someone whose headline says "Former Chef" creates curiosity gaps
- **Strong POV with credentials** — Opinions backed by visible experience make people want to learn more about the author
- **Teased depth** — "This is one of 7 patterns I've identified" implies there's more to discover on the profile
- **Pattern interrupts** — Content that doesn't match the reader's expectations of "typical LinkedIn posts" creates intrigue

### What Kills It

- **Generic advice anyone could give** — If the post could be written by anyone, there's no reason to check who wrote it
- **No personal signal** — Posts that read like textbook content don't create curiosity about the author
- **Complete information** — If everything is in the post, there's no reason to visit the profile for more
- **Weak or missing author context** — If there's no line like "after 5 years of doing X" or "as someone who built Y," the reader has no anchor for curiosity
- **Commodity takes** — "AI is the future" doesn't make anyone wonder about you specifically

### How to Improve a Low Score

1. Add one line of author context — a specific credential, experience, or result that grounds your authority
2. Tease additional depth ("I've written about the other 4 patterns on my profile")
3. Reference a unique vantage point — what do you see that others in your position don't?
4. Include a detail that only someone with real experience would know
5. Make sure your headline and profile support the expertise the post implies — a profile click that leads to a weak profile wastes the opportunity

## Dimension 5: Identity Resonance

**Score range:** 1–5

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This is the biggest missing variable in most content systems.** Most viral LinkedIn content succeeds not because of formatting tricks or posting times, but because people share it to signal who they are.

### The Three Identity Triggers

People engage with content that helps them express identity in one of three ways:

#### 1. Validation — "This Is Me"
The reader sees themselves in the post and engages to say "yes, this is my experience too."

- **Example:** "Introverts don't hate networking. We hate performative networking." → Every introvert likes and comments because it validates their identity
- **How to trigger it:** Describe a specific, slightly hidden experience that a group has but rarely sees articulated

#### 2. Belonging — "I'm Part of This Tribe"
The reader engages to signal membership in a group they identify with.

- **Example:** "Solo founders don't have the luxury of 'move fast and break things.' We move fast and fix things ourselves." → Every solo founder reposts this
- **How to trigger it:** Name the tribe explicitly. Use "we" language. Describe shared struggles or values that outsiders wouldn't understand

#### 3. Aspiration — "I'm the Kind of Person Who Thinks This Way"
The reader engages because sharing the post signals the type of person they want to be seen as.

- **Example:** "I'd rather build a $500K/year business with freedom than a $5M/year business with a board." → Lifestyle entrepreneurs repost to signal their values
- **How to trigger it:** Articulate a value or choice that a specific group aspires to. Make it feel like a stance, not advice

### What Kills Identity Resonance

- **Universal statements** — "Everyone should work smarter, not harder" resonates with no specific group
- **Detached analysis** — Academic observations don't help anyone express who they are
- **Advice without values** — "Use this tool to save time" is useful but identity-neutral
- **Trying to appeal to everyone** — The more universal you go, the less any specific group claims it as "theirs"

### How to Improve a Low Score

1. Pick ONE specific tribe (founders, operators, career changers, AI builders, freelancers) and write directly to them
2. Use "we" instead of "you" — shift from advice-giver to fellow traveler
3. Articulate a value or belief that the tribe holds but rarely sees in writing
4. Include a line that an outsider wouldn't fully understand but an insider would nod at
5. Ask: "Would someone screenshot this and post it to their story/slack with 'THIS'?" If yes, identity resonance is high

## Quick Decision Matrix

| Total Score | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| 20–25 | Publish immediately. This has strong viral mechanics. |
| 16–19 | Publish after addressing the weakest dimension. Solid post. |
| 12–15 | Revise significantly. At least 2 dimensions need work. Consider a different angle or format. |
| Below 12 | Rethink the post. The core idea may not be distribution-ready. Save the insight for a better format. |

---

## Audit Workflow

1. **Draft the post** through the normal pipeline (Insight → Positioning → Narrative → Format → Polish)
2. **Score each dimension** honestly — don't inflate scores
3. **Identify the weakest dimension** — improve it using the "How to Improve" section
4. **Re-score** after revisions
5. **Publish** if ≥ 16 with no dimension below 2
6. **After publishing**, log actual performance in the Learning Layer and compare predicted scores vs. real engagement
