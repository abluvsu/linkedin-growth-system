# ICP Engine

Every post is written for a specific reader. Before drafting, select the primary ICP and design the post to match their attention triggers, engagement drivers, and language.

---

## ICP Profiles

### Founder

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What they scroll for** | Fundraising signals, growth tactics, founder war stories, hiring strategies, competitive intelligence. |
| **What makes them stop** | Specific numbers (revenue, burn rate, conversion %). A decision they're currently facing described with uncomfortable accuracy. A contrarian take on advice they've been following. |
| **What makes them engage** | Posts that validate a lonely decision they made. Posts that challenge advice from people who've never operated. Posts where they can add their own war story in the comments. |
| **Language they use** | "Burn rate," "runway," "PMF," "iterate," "default alive," "cap table," "we shipped," "I had to let someone go," "the board said." Direct, compressed, low tolerance for fluff. |

### Product Manager

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What they scroll for** | Prioritisation frameworks, stakeholder management tactics, metrics that matter, AI product implications, career progression into leadership. |
| **What makes them stop** | A specific example of a hard prioritisation call and its outcome. A framework they haven't seen before. Evidence that challenges how they currently measure success. |
| **What makes them engage** | Posts that acknowledge the political reality of PM work (not just the craft). Posts where the answer isn't obvious and they want to weigh in. Posts about the gap between what PMs are taught and what actually works. |
| **Language they use** | "Trade-offs," "roadmap," "stakeholder alignment," "north star metric," "user stories," "we de-scoped," "discovery vs delivery," "impact vs effort." Structured, analytical, framework-oriented. |

### Operator

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What they scroll for** | Process improvements, automation opportunities, team scaling patterns, tool comparisons, operational efficiency stories. |
| **What makes them stop** | A before/after process improvement with measurable results. A hidden inefficiency they recognise in their own organisation. A tool or workflow they haven't considered. |
| **What makes them engage** | Posts that respect the complexity of execution (not just strategy). Posts where they can share their own process hack. Posts that surface a problem everyone experiences but nobody talks about. |
| **Language they use** | "SOP," "bottleneck," "throughput," "handoff," "we automated," "the process broke when," "single point of failure," "edge case." Precise, systems-oriented, allergic to vagueness. |

### Job Seeker

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What they scroll for** | Application strategies, interview prep, salary negotiation, resume advice, signals about which companies are hiring, career pivot stories. |
| **What makes them stop** | Insider knowledge about how hiring actually works (vs. how candidates think it works). Specific before/after examples of application materials. A hiring manager revealing what they actually look at. |
| **What makes them engage** | Posts that reduce anxiety by giving them a concrete step. Posts that make them feel seen in a process that often feels dehumanising. Posts where they can ask a follow-up question without feeling judged. |
| **Language they use** | "Applied to 200 jobs," "ghosted," "ATS," "cover letter," "final round," "offer negotiation," "career pivot," "transferable skills." Emotionally charged, hope-seeking, action-oriented. |

### Consultant

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What they scroll for** | Client acquisition strategies, pricing models, scope management, thought leadership examples, niche positioning, proposal frameworks. |
| **What makes them stop** | A specific client situation described with enough detail to feel real. Revenue or pricing data from someone at a similar stage. A positioning insight that makes them rethink their own niche. |
| **What makes them engage** | Posts about the business side of consulting (not just the craft). Posts that acknowledge the feast-or-famine cycle without romanticising it. Posts where sharing their own experience would demonstrate expertise to potential clients reading the comments. |
| **Language they use** | "Retainer," "scope creep," "day rate," "pipeline," "discovery call," "proposal," "SOW," "niche down," "inbound vs outbound," "they asked for a discount." Business-fluent, independent-minded, pragmatic. |

### Mid-Career Professional

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What they scroll for** | Leadership transition advice, skill relevance in an AI-disrupted market, promotion strategies, work-life integration, stories from people 2-3 years ahead of them. |
| **What makes them stop** | A post that names the specific anxiety they feel but haven't articulated (obsolescence, plateau, identity tied to title). Evidence that a lateral move or unconventional path worked out. A new way to think about their experience as an asset, not a liability. |
| **What makes them engage** | Posts that treat their experience as valuable (not outdated). Posts that give them a framework to evaluate their next move. Posts where commenting signals their seniority and thoughtfulness to their own network. |
| **Language they use** | "Next chapter," "reinvention," "my experience tells me," "I've seen this before," "leadership pipeline," "visibility," "executive presence," "I'm not sure the old playbook works anymore." Reflective, measured, seeking validation and direction simultaneously. |

## Think / Feel / Do Framework

For every post, define the desired reader reaction across three dimensions before writing.

### Definitions

| Dimension | Question | Options |
|---|---|---|
| **Think** | What new belief should the reader form? | A specific, articulable idea they didn't hold before reading. |
| **Feel** | What emotion should they experience? | Urgency, validation, curiosity, relief, discomfort, hope, recognition. |
| **Do** | What action should they take? | Follow, comment, save, share, click profile, DM, apply a framework. |

### Rules

1. **Think must be specific.** "They should think about their career" is too vague. "They should believe that interview performance is a skill separate from job performance" is specific enough.
2. **Feel must be honest.** Do not manufacture false urgency or fear. The emotion should be a natural response to the insight.
3. **Do must be realistic.** Not every post earns a DM. Match the action to the depth of the post.

## Example Mapping: CareerFlow Post

**Post Topic:** Why the best candidates often fail final-round interviews.

**Primary ICP:** Job Seeker

**Think / Feel / Do:**

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---|
| **Think** | "Final-round interviews test a different skill than earlier rounds. I need to prepare differently for each stage, not treat them all the same." |
| **Feel** | Relief ("So it's not just me — the process is genuinely different at each stage") and urgency ("I need to adjust my approach before my next final round"). |
| **Do** | Save the post for reference before their next interview cycle. Comment with their own final-round experience. Follow for more hiring-process insights. |

### Why This Mapping Works

- **Think** gives the Job Seeker a belief they can act on immediately, not just a vague reassurance.
- **Feel** combines relief (reduces the shame of past rejections) with urgency (motivates behaviour change). Neither emotion is manufactured — both follow naturally from the insight.
- **Do** is calibrated to the ICP's behaviour: Job Seekers save practical content at a high rate, and commenting about their own experience in a safe context is a low-friction engagement action.
